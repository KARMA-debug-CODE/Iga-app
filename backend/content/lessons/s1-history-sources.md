## What is history?

**History** is the study of past human activities. Historians are like detectives: they collect evidence from **sources** and use it to explain what happened and why.

## Primary and secondary sources

- A **primary source** was made at the time being studied, by people who were there: a letter, a photograph, a tool, an eyewitness account.
- A **secondary source** was made later by someone studying the past: a textbook, a documentary, an article.

## Main sources of history

### 1. Oral tradition
Stories, songs, poems, proverbs and genealogies passed down by word of mouth. In Rwanda, oral tradition — including dynastic poetry and stories told by elders — kept knowledge of the past alive for centuries.

- **Strengths:** available where nothing was written; shows people's values.
- **Weaknesses:** details may change as stories are retold; dates can be unclear.

### 2. Written sources
Books, letters, diaries, newspapers, government records, travellers' accounts.

- **Strengths:** can give exact dates and names.
- **Weaknesses:** the writer may be biased or misunderstand what they saw.

### 3. Archaeology
The study of material remains — tools, pottery, bones, buildings — dug from the ground. Scientists use methods like **carbon dating** to find their age.

- **Strengths:** gives evidence from times before writing.
- **Weaknesses:** expensive; objects don't explain themselves.

### 4. Other sources
Museums (such as the Ethnographic Museum in Huye), historical sites, linguistics (how languages are related), photographs, films and the internet.

## Is the source reliable?

Ask these questions about any source:

1. **Who** made it, and were they there?
2. **When** was it made?
3. **Why** was it made — to inform, persuade, or entertain?
4. Does it **agree** with other sources?

Using several sources that agree (called **corroboration**) gives a more reliable picture.
