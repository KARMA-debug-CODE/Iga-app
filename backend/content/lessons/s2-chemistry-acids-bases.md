## Acids and bases around you

| Acids | Bases / alkalis |
|---|---|
| lemon juice, vinegar, sour milk | soap, wood ash, toothpaste |
| taste sour (never taste chemicals in the lab!) | feel soapy |
| turn blue litmus **red** | turn red litmus **blue** |
| contain $\text{H}^+$ ions in water | alkalis contain $\text{OH}^-$ ions in water |

An **alkali** is a base that dissolves in water.

## The pH scale

The pH scale runs from 0 to 14:

- **pH < 7** acidic (the lower, the stronger)
- **pH = 7** neutral (pure water)
- **pH > 7** alkaline (the higher, the stronger)

| pH | Example |
|---|---|
| 1 | stomach acid |
| 3 | orange juice |
| 7 | pure water |
| 8 | sea water |
| 11 | ammonia solution |
| 13 | oven cleaner |

Universal indicator shows the pH with a colour: red (strong acid) → green (neutral) → purple (strong alkali).

## Neutralisation

An acid and a base cancel each other out:

$$\text{acid} + \text{base} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water}$$

$$\text{HCl} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Uses in daily life:

- Farmers add **lime** (a base) to acidic soils to improve crops.
- **Antacid** tablets neutralise excess stomach acid.
- Toothpaste neutralises acids made by bacteria in your mouth.
