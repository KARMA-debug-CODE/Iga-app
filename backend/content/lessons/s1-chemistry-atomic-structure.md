## Three sub-atomic particles

Everything is made of atoms, and every atom is made of three kinds of particle:

| Particle | Where it is | Relative charge | Relative mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| proton | nucleus | $+1$ | 1 |
| neutron | nucleus | 0 | 1 |
| electron | shells around the nucleus | $-1$ | $\frac{1}{1840}$ (almost 0) |

The **nucleus** is tiny but contains almost all the mass. An atom is mostly empty space.

## Atomic number and mass number

- **Atomic number** $Z$ = number of protons. It decides **which element** the atom is.
- **Mass number** $A$ = protons + neutrons.

We write them like this:

$$^{A}_{Z}\text{X} \qquad \text{e.g. } ^{23}_{11}\text{Na}$$

Sodium has 11 protons, $23 - 11 = 12$ neutrons, and (in a neutral atom) 11 electrons.

## Electron shells

Electrons fill shells from the inside out. For the first 20 elements the shells hold **2, 8, 8, 2**.

| Element | $Z$ | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon | 6 | 2, 4 |
| Oxygen | 8 | 2, 6 |
| Sodium | 11 | 2, 8, 1 |
| Chlorine | 17 | 2, 8, 7 |
| Calcium | 20 | 2, 8, 8, 2 |

The electrons in the **outer shell** decide how an element reacts.

## Isotopes

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element with **different numbers of neutrons**. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 both have 6 protons, but 6 and 8 neutrons. Carbon-14 is used to date ancient objects.

## Ions

If an atom loses electrons it becomes a **positive ion** (sodium → $\text{Na}^+$). If it gains electrons it becomes a **negative ion** (chlorine → $\text{Cl}^-$).
