## Words have jobs

Every word in a sentence does a job. Grammar books group these jobs into **eight parts of speech**.

| Part of speech | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun** | names a person, place, thing or idea | Kigali, teacher, football, honesty |
| **Pronoun** | replaces a noun | she, they, it, us |
| **Verb** | shows an action or state | run, write, is, seem |
| **Adjective** | describes a noun | tall, green, three, beautiful |
| **Adverb** | describes a verb, adjective or another adverb | quickly, very, often, yesterday |
| **Preposition** | shows position or relationship | in, on, under, between |
| **Conjunction** | joins words or clauses | and, but, because, although |
| **Interjection** | shows sudden feeling | Wow! Oh! Ouch! |

## See them in a sentence

> **Wow**, **my** **young** **brother** **ran** **very** **quickly** **to** **school** **and** **arrived** **early**.

- Wow — interjection
- my — pronoun (possessive determiner)
- young — adjective
- brother, school — nouns
- ran, arrived — verbs
- very, quickly, early — adverbs
- to — preposition
- and — conjunction

## One word, different jobs

The same word can change its part of speech:

- I *drink* milk every morning. (**verb**)
- Would you like a *drink*? (**noun**)
- She ran *fast*. (**adverb**) — a *fast* car (**adjective**)

So always ask: *what is this word doing in this sentence?*

## Common mistakes

- *Good* is an adjective; *well* is the adverb: "She sings **well**" (not *good*).
- Adverbs often end in **-ly**, but not all do (*fast, hard, soon*), and some -ly words are adjectives (*friendly, lovely*).

## Check yourself

Name the part of speech of each word in italics:

1. The children played *happily* in the field.
2. Uwase is clever *but* lazy.
3. The book is *under* the desk.
4. *They* visited Akagera National Park.

*Answers: 1) adverb · 2) conjunction · 3) preposition · 4) pronoun*
