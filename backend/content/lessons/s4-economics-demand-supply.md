## Demand

**Demand** is the quantity of a good buyers are *willing and able* to buy at each price, in a given time.

**Law of demand:** other things being equal (*ceteris paribus*), when the price **rises**, quantity demanded **falls**. The demand curve slopes **downwards**.

What **shifts** the whole demand curve?

- income of consumers
- prices of related goods (substitutes and complements)
- tastes and fashion
- population size
- expectations about future prices

## Supply

**Supply** is the quantity sellers are willing and able to offer at each price.

**Law of supply:** when the price **rises**, quantity supplied **rises**. The supply curve slopes **upwards**.

What **shifts** supply?

- costs of production (wages, fertiliser, fuel)
- technology
- weather (very important for farm products)
- taxes and subsidies
- number of sellers

## Equilibrium

The market is in **equilibrium** where quantity demanded = quantity supplied. At that price there is no pressure to change.

- Price **above** equilibrium → **surplus** (excess supply) → sellers cut prices.
- Price **below** equilibrium → **shortage** (excess demand) → prices are pushed up.

**Example with equations.** $Q_d = 100 - 2P$ and $Q_s = 20 + 2P$.

$$100 - 2P = 20 + 2P \Rightarrow 4P = 80 \Rightarrow P = 20,\ Q = 60$$
