## Distance and displacement

- **Distance** is how far you travel along your path. It has size only (a **scalar**).
- **Displacement** is how far you end up from where you started, *in a straight line, with a direction* (a **vector**).

If you walk 300 m to school and 300 m back home, your distance is 600 m but your displacement is **0 m**.

## Speed

$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} \qquad v = \frac{d}{t}$$

The SI unit is metres per second (m/s). Road signs use km/h. To convert:

$$1\ \text{m/s} = 3.6\ \text{km/h}$$

**Example.** A bus travels 120 km from Kigali to Huye in 2.5 h. Its average speed is $\frac{120}{2.5} = 48$ km/h, which is $48 \div 3.6 \approx 13.3$ m/s.

## Velocity

**Velocity** is speed *in a stated direction*: 15 m/s north is a velocity; 15 m/s is a speed. Two cars moving at 60 km/h in opposite directions have the same speed but different velocities.

## Acceleration

Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes:

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

where $u$ is the starting velocity and $v$ the final velocity. The unit is m/s² ("metres per second, every second").

**Example.** A moto speeds up from rest to 12 m/s in 4 s:

$$a = \frac{12 - 0}{4} = 3\ \text{m/s}^2$$

A **negative** acceleration (deceleration) means the object is slowing down.

## Distance–time graphs

- A **straight sloping line** → constant speed. The gradient is the speed.
- A **flat line** → the object is stationary.
- A **curve getting steeper** → the object is speeding up.
