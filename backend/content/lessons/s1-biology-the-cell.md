## All living things are made of cells

The **cell** is the smallest unit that can carry out all the processes of life. Some organisms, like bacteria and amoeba, are a single cell. You are made of about 37 trillion cells.

Most cells are too small to see without a **microscope**.

## Parts of a cell

| Part | Job | Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell membrane | controls what enters and leaves | yes | yes |
| Cytoplasm | jelly where most reactions happen | yes | yes |
| Nucleus | contains DNA; controls the cell | yes | yes |
| Mitochondria | release energy by respiration | yes | yes |
| Ribosomes | make proteins | yes | yes |
| Cell wall | made of cellulose; gives shape and support | no | yes |
| Chloroplasts | contain chlorophyll; photosynthesis | no | yes |
| Large permanent vacuole | holds cell sap; keeps the cell firm | no | yes |

> **Memory trick:** plants have the **3 Cs**: **C**ell wall, **C**hloroplasts and a big **C**entral vacuole.

## Specialised cells

Cells change their shape to do a particular job:

- **Red blood cells** have no nucleus and a dimpled shape, giving more room for haemoglobin to carry oxygen.
- **Root hair cells** have a long extension to absorb water from the soil.
- **Nerve cells** are very long to carry messages around the body.
- **Sperm cells** have a tail to swim.

## Levels of organisation

$$\text{cell} \rightarrow \text{tissue} \rightarrow \text{organ} \rightarrow \text{organ system} \rightarrow \text{organism}$$

Example: muscle cells → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → human.

## Practical: onion cells

1. Peel a thin layer from inside an onion.
2. Place it flat on a slide with a drop of iodine solution.
3. Lower a cover slip at an angle to avoid air bubbles.
4. Observe at low then high power. Draw and label what you see.
