## Why do we digest food?

A plate of **ubugari**, **isombe** and **ibiharage** (beans) contains starch, protein and fat. These are **large, insoluble molecules**. They cannot pass through the wall of the gut into the blood.

**Digestion** is the breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood.

Food passes through five stages:

1. **Ingestion**: taking food into the mouth.
2. **Digestion**: breaking food down, both **mechanically** (teeth, churning) and **chemically** (enzymes).
3. **Absorption**: small molecules pass through the gut wall into the blood.
4. **Assimilation**: body cells use the absorbed molecules.
5. **Egestion**: undigested food (faeces) leaves through the anus.

## Digestive enzymes

**Enzymes** are proteins that speed up chemical reactions without being used up. Each enzyme works on one type of food.

| Enzyme | Works on | Products | Made in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | Maltose (a sugar) | Salivary glands, pancreas |
| Protease (pepsin, trypsin) | Protein | Shorter chains, then amino acids | Stomach, pancreas |
| Lipase | Fats and oils | Fatty acids and glycerol | Pancreas |

Human enzymes work best at body temperature, about 37 °C. Very high temperatures **denature** them: their shape is destroyed and they stop working.

## The journey through the gut

### Mouth

**Teeth** cut and grind food into small pieces, giving a larger surface area for enzymes. **Saliva** moistens the food and contains **amylase**, which starts digesting starch. The tongue rolls the food into a ball called a **bolus**.

There are four kinds of teeth:

- **Incisors**: chisel-shaped, for cutting and biting
- **Canines**: pointed, for tearing
- **Premolars** and **molars**: broad, for crushing and grinding

### Worked example 1: counting teeth from a dental formula

A **dental formula** shows the teeth in **one half** of the upper jaw (top number) and lower jaw (bottom number). For an adult human:

$$i\,\frac{2}{2}\quad c\,\frac{1}{1}\quad pm\,\frac{2}{2}\quad m\,\frac{3}{3}$$

1. Upper teeth on one side: $2 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 8$
2. Lower teeth on one side: $2 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 8$
3. One side of the mouth: $8 + 8 = 16$
4. Both sides: $16 \times 2 = 32$ teeth

A young child has milk teeth $i\,\frac{2}{2}\ c\,\frac{1}{1}\ m\,\frac{2}{2}$, so $(5 + 5) \times 2 = 20$ teeth.

### Oesophagus

The bolus is pushed down the **oesophagus** by **peristalsis**: waves of muscle contraction behind the food. Peristalsis moves food along the whole gut, so you can even swallow while upside down.

### Stomach

The muscular stomach wall **churns** food for a few hours, turning it into a liquid called **chyme**. Gastric juice contains:

- **Hydrochloric acid**, which kills most bacteria in food and makes conditions acidic (about pH 2)
- **Pepsin**, a protease that starts digesting proteins and works best in acid

### Duodenum

The first part of the small intestine receives two juices:

- **Bile** is made in the **liver** and stored in the **gall bladder**. It neutralises stomach acid and **emulsifies** fats, breaking them into tiny droplets so lipase can act on a larger surface area. Bile contains no enzymes.
- **Pancreatic juice** contains amylase, trypsin (a protease) and lipase.

### Ileum

In the ileum, enzymes made by the gut wall complete digestion. For example, **maltase** turns maltose into **glucose**. The products are then **absorbed**. The small intestine is about 6 m long, and the inner wall of the ileum is covered with millions of tiny finger-like **villi**. Each villus has:

- a wall only **one cell thick**, so molecules diffuse a short distance
- a dense network of **blood capillaries**, which take glucose and amino acids to the liver
- a **lacteal**, which absorbs fatty acids and glycerol
- **microvilli** on its cells, adding even more surface area

### Large intestine

The **colon** absorbs most of the remaining **water** and some mineral salts. Faeces are stored in the **rectum** and egested through the **anus**. Fibre from vegetables, fruit and beans helps the gut muscles push food along and prevents constipation.

### Worked example 2: tracing a protein

Follow the protein in a plate of beans:

1. Mouth: chewed into small pieces; no protein digestion yet.
2. Stomach: pepsin breaks proteins into shorter chains.
3. Duodenum: trypsin from the pancreas continues the breakdown.
4. Ileum: enzymes in the gut wall finish the job, producing **amino acids**.
5. Amino acids are absorbed into the blood capillaries of the villi and carried to the liver, then to cells that use them to build new proteins (**assimilation**).

## Investigating amylase

Mix starch solution with amylase in test tubes kept at different temperatures. Every 30 seconds, put a drop of the mixture onto **iodine solution** on a spotting tile. Iodine turns **blue-black** while starch is present and stays **orange-brown** once all the starch is digested.

### Worked example 3: comparing rates

A class in Rubavu records the time for the starch to disappear.

| Temperature | Time for starch to disappear |
|---|---|
| 15 °C | 12 minutes |
| 37 °C | 3 minutes |
| 80 °C | Starch never disappears |

The rate of digestion can be measured as $\dfrac{1}{\text{time}}$.

- At 15 °C: rate $= \dfrac{1}{12} \approx 0.083$ per minute
- At 37 °C: rate $= \dfrac{1}{3} \approx 0.33$ per minute
- Comparison: $\dfrac{12}{3} = 4$, so digestion is **4 times faster** at 37 °C.

At 80 °C the amylase has been denatured, so no starch is digested.

## Safety in the lab

- Wear **goggles**. Iodine solution stains skin and clothes and is harmful if swallowed.
- **Never taste** food or solutions in the laboratory.
- If you use saliva as a source of amylase, use only your own and rinse all glassware with hot soapy water afterwards.
- Heat test tubes in a water bath, not directly in a flame, and point them away from people.

## Keeping the gut healthy

Wash your hands before eating, drink boiled or treated water, and eat plenty of fibre. Germs in dirty water cause diarrhoea, which makes the colon lose water instead of absorbing it.
