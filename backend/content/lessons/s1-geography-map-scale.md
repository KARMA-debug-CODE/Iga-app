## Why maps need a scale

A map is a reduced drawing of an area. The **scale** tells you how much smaller the map is than the real ground. Without a scale you cannot measure real distances.

## Three ways to show scale

1. **Statement scale** — in words: "1 cm represents 1 km".
2. **Representative fraction (RF)** — a ratio with no units: **1 : 50 000** means 1 unit on the map is 50 000 of the same units on the ground.
3. **Linear (bar) scale** — a ruler-like line divided into distances.

```
0        1        2        3 km
├────────┼────────┼────────┤
```

## Converting between scales

Remember: $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m} = 100\,000\ \text{cm}$.

**RF → statement.** 1 : 50 000 means 1 cm represents 50 000 cm = **0.5 km**.

**Statement → RF.** "1 cm represents 2 km" → 2 km = 200 000 cm → **1 : 200 000**.

## Measuring distance

**Straight line:** measure with a ruler, then multiply by the scale.

> On a 1 : 50 000 map, two villages are 6 cm apart. Real distance $= 6 \times 0.5 = 3$ km.

**Curved line (road or river):** lay a piece of string along the route, mark the ends, straighten it against a ruler, then convert. Or step along the curve with the edge of a paper, marking each straight section.

## Large and small scale

- **Large-scale** maps (e.g. 1 : 10 000) show a small area in great detail — a town plan of Musanze.
- **Small-scale** maps (e.g. 1 : 1 000 000) show a large area with little detail — a map of East Africa.

A good way to remember: in 1 : 10 000 the *fraction* $\frac{1}{10\,000}$ is **larger** than $\frac{1}{1\,000\,000}$.

## Check yourself

1. Convert 1 : 25 000 to a statement scale in km.
2. On a 1 : 100 000 map, a road measures 7.5 cm. How long is it?
3. Which shows more detail: 1 : 5 000 or 1 : 500 000?

*Answers: 1) 1 cm represents 0.25 km · 2) 7.5 km · 3) 1 : 5 000*
