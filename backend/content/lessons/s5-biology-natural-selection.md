## Darwin's idea

Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace proposed that species change over time by **natural selection**. The argument has four steps:

1. **Variation** — individuals in a population differ (size, colour, resistance to disease…). Much of this variation is caused by genes.
2. **Over-production and competition** — more offspring are born than can survive, so they compete for food, space and mates.
3. **Survival of the best adapted** — individuals with helpful variations are more likely to survive and reproduce.
4. **Inheritance** — they pass the helpful alleles to their offspring. Over many generations, those alleles become more common.

## Where does variation come from?

- **Mutations** — random changes in DNA create new alleles.
- **Sexual reproduction** — mixes alleles from two parents (meiosis and random fertilisation).

## Examples

- **Antibiotic-resistant bacteria.** When antibiotics are used, the few resistant bacteria survive and multiply. This is why you must finish a course of antibiotics.
- **Malaria and drug resistance.** *Plasmodium* has evolved resistance to some anti-malarial drugs, so treatments must change over time.
- **The peppered moth.** In polluted industrial areas, dark moths were better camouflaged on sooty trees and became more common.

## Selection pressures

Anything that affects survival is a **selection pressure**: predators, disease, climate, food supply. If the environment changes, different variations become advantageous.

## Evidence for evolution

- Fossils showing gradual change
- Similar bone structures in different species (homologous structures)
- DNA comparisons between species
- Directly observed evolution in bacteria and insects
