## Why balance?

In a chemical reaction atoms are **rearranged**, not created or destroyed. This is the **law of conservation of mass**. A balanced equation shows the same number of each type of atom on both sides.

## Word equation → symbol equation

Hydrogen burns in oxygen to make water:

$$\text{hydrogen} + \text{oxygen} \rightarrow \text{water}$$

$$\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \text{(not balanced)}$$

Count: left has 2 O, right has 1 O.

## The method

1. Write the correct formulae. **Never change a formula** to balance (H₂O must stay H₂O).
2. Count each type of atom on both sides.
3. Put **numbers in front** (coefficients) to balance one element at a time.
4. Leave elements that appear on their own (like O₂ or Fe) until last.
5. Check every element again.

For hydrogen and oxygen: put 2 in front of H₂O to balance O, then 2 in front of H₂ to balance H:

$$2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Check: H: 4 = 4 ✓, O: 2 = 2 ✓.

## More examples

$$\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

$$\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{NH}_3$$

$$2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}$$

## State symbols

We often add the state of each substance: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

$$\text{Zn(s)} + 2\text{HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$
